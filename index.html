02 / CARDIAC TISSUE ENGINEERING / GROSBERG LAB

Heart-on-a-chip Research

I develop and characterize in vitro cardiac injury models using neonatal rat ventricular myocytes (NRVMs) cultured on micropatterned PDMS heart-on-a-chip platforms. My work includes device fabrication using PDMS spin coating and laser cutting, assisting with cardiomyocyte harvests and culture, running ischemia-reperfusion experiments, live/dead staining and fluorescence microscopy, and muscular thin-film contractility measurements. For quantitative analysis, I work with custom Cellpose models for automated nuclear segmentation and cell-viability quantification across experimental conditions, and process contractility data in ImageJ. As a Lab Manager Assistant for the Cardiovascular Innovation and Research Center (CIRC), I also support shared research operations across multiple laboratories by managing common-space inventory and equipment readiness, maintaining biosafety cabinets, microscopes, autoclaves, and vacuum systems, and monitoring liquid-nitrogen storage for frozen cell stocks.

03 / MEDICAL DEVICE DESIGN / ERGONOMIC AUTOINJECTOR

Ergonomic Autoinjector for Rheumatoid Arthritis

Our team developed a button-free, spring-actuated autoinjector concept for patients with rheumatoid arthritis, designed to reduce the grip strength, button pressing, and twisting motions required by conventional devices. The mechanism combines an ergonomic grip, spring-driven injection, and a deployable needle cover to support steadier use for patients with limited hand dexterity. My work focused on CAD design of the 3D-printed components, physical prototyping, and assembly, integrating the syringe, springs, housing, needle-cover mechanism, and ergonomic grip while iterating component fit and mechanism performance across multiple printed versions.

04 / MEDICAL DEVICE DESIGN / ELECTROMECHANICAL SYSTEMS

Automated Medication Dispenser

Our team developed a 3D-printed automated medication dispenser designed to support medication adherence through programmable dosing and reminders. The concept combines a servo-driven rotating compartment system with Arduino control, push-button schedule selection, LED indicators, and audible alerts. The control logic supports daily, weekly, and biweekly dispensing schedules, with the servo advancing the medication wheel and the system updating visual and audio cues throughout the cycle. We iterated the mechanical design to improve servo placement and compartment layout, then produced a physical prototype alongside a functioning electronics and control-system proof of concept.

05 / ROBOTICS & AUTONOMY / ENGR 7B / TEAM CAPTAIN

Autonomous Rover

I led a seven-person team developing an autonomous rover designed to follow a line, identify a colored target, and retrieve it using a servo-actuated claw. As Team Captain, I managed project scheduling and documentation while co-developing the rover’s control code, integrating three IR sensors for PID-based line following with PixyCam object tracking and claw control. Through subsystem testing and debugging, we independently validated line-following and color-tracking behavior while working through hardware failures and final software-integration challenges.

06 / MECHANICAL DESIGN / VEHICLE STEERING

Reverse Ackermann Steering Rover

As one of two SolidWorks designers, I co-designed the chassis and reverse Ackermann steering system for a remote-controlled rover developed over ten weeks. The design used a compact 4 × 11.5 in chassis, 85 mm wheels, a 52-tooth gear, and angled steering linkages to balance stability, traction, and maneuverability. We iterated the steering geometry through multiple 3D-printed revisions as fabrication exposed fit and clearance constraints, and the completed rover successfully navigated the final course in 2 minutes, 33 seconds.

07 / MEDTECH INNOVATION / PROGRAM DESIGN & LEADERSHIP

MedTech Founders Program

It started with three of us, a whiteboard, and an idea to build a community where students could turn their ideas in MedTech into something real.

I co-founded and manage UCI BMES’s MedTech Founders Program, a yearlong, three-phase pathway serving 50+ students and guiding teams from identifying unmet medical needs through concept development, entrepreneurship, and prototyping. As Project Manager, I design curriculum and engineering activities, coordinate workshops and technical resources, mentor project teams, and build opportunities for structured feedback from peers, faculty, graduate students, alumni, and industry. The program is designed to make medical-device development more accessible while giving students hands-on experience turning early ideas into increasingly developed engineering solutions. In its first year, three MFP teams went on to win student design competitions across campus, and two teams submitted their projects to the National BMES Student Design Competition.
